Core concepts

Use these skills in English and every other subject.

Writing

Thesis statement

Definition: A clear, arguable central claim answering the task and guiding the response.

It should be specific enough to organize evidence and broad enough to support several reasons.

Example: The novel presents ambition as destructive when separated from responsibility.
Common mistake: Writing only a topic announcement.
Writing

Evidence-based paragraph

Definition: A paragraph that makes a point, supports it, explains the evidence, and links to the argument.

PEEL, PEE, CER, and similar structures all require reasoning, not just quotation.

Example: Point → evidence → interpretation → connection.
Common mistake: Dropping in evidence without explaining why it matters.
Reading

Inference

Definition: A conclusion supported by textual clues and relevant background knowledge.

An inference must be plausible and traceable to evidence.

Example: Repeatedly avoiding questions may suggest discomfort or concealment.
Common mistake: Treating an unsupported guess as an inference.
Reading

Tone

Definition: The writer’s or speaker’s attitude toward the subject or audience.

Infer tone through diction, imagery, structure, emphasis, and context.

Example: Words such as “reckless” and “avoidable” create a critical tone.
Common mistake: Confusing tone with the reader’s mood.
Media literacy

Bias and framing

Definition: The influence of perspective, selection, emphasis, language, or interests on presentation.

Identify bias, then corroborate factual claims with other reliable evidence.

Example: Two headlines may frame the same protest as disruption or civic action.
Common mistake: Calling every opposing viewpoint “fake.”
Research

Paraphrasing

Definition: Restating an idea fully in your own wording and structure while citing the source.

Read, close the source, explain from understanding, verify accuracy, and cite.

Example: A genuine paraphrase changes both wording and sentence structure.
Common mistake: Changing only a few words.
Argument

Counterargument

Definition: A relevant objection or alternative position considered fairly and answered with reasoning.

Accurate counterarguments strengthen credibility and analysis.

Example: A policy essay may acknowledge costs before explaining long-term benefits.
Common mistake: Misrepresenting the other side.
✍️

Writing process

A repeatable method for essays and reports.

  1. Analyze the task and command words.
  2. Form an arguable thesis.
  3. Organize evidence into reasons.
  4. Write evidence-based paragraphs.
  5. Consider relevant alternatives.
  6. Revise reasoning and organization.
  7. Edit grammar, citation, and formatting.
📝

Literacy preparation

Practise reading informational, narrative, and graphic texts and producing clear, organized writing supported by relevant details.

Study method: Complete timed practice, review why each answer is correct, and keep an error log by skill—not just by question.
