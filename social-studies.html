History, Geography & Civics

Definitions, examples, inquiry frameworks, and common mistakes for Canadian and world studies.

Curriculum note: Ontario Canadian and world studies courses emphasize inquiry, geographic and historical thinking, political thinking, Indigenous perspectives, and informed citizenship.
📜

History

Interpret evidence, context, cause, consequence, continuity, change, perspectives, and ethics.

Historical thinking

Historical significance

Definition: The importance assigned to an event, person, development, or idea.

Judge significance using impact, duration, relevance, and what it reveals about the past.

Example: Confederation is significant because it changed political structures and relationships across the land.
Common mistake: Assuming an event is significant only because it is famous.
Evidence

Primary source

Definition: Evidence created during the period studied or by a direct participant or witness.

Evaluate creator, purpose, audience, context, limitations, and corroboration.

Example: A 1917 diary can reveal one soldier’s wartime experience.
Common mistake: Treating a primary source as automatically unbiased.
Historical thinking

Cause and consequence

Definition: The relationship between conditions or actions and the results that followed.

Distinguish long-term conditions, immediate triggers, intended effects, and unintended consequences.

Example: Industrialization increased production and also contributed to urban crowding and labour conflict.
Common mistake: Explaining a complex event with only one cause.
Historical thinking

Continuity and change

Definition: What remained similar and what changed across time.

Consider pace, direction, turning points, and differences among groups.

Example: Voting rights expanded over time, but barriers did not disappear at once.
Common mistake: Assuming change always means progress.
Canada

Treaties and treaty relationships

Definition: Agreements between Indigenous nations and the Crown establishing ongoing rights, responsibilities, and relationships.

Study written and oral understandings, historical context, implementation, and continuing legal and moral responsibilities.

Example: A treaty may address land sharing, governance, harvesting, payments, and mutual obligations.
Common mistake: Describing treaties only as past land sales.
Canada

Canadian Confederation

Definition: The 1867 union that created the Dominion of Canada from four provinces.

Examine political compromise, economics, defence, federalism, expansion, and effects on peoples excluded from negotiations.

Example: The Constitution Act, 1867 divided powers between federal and provincial governments.
Common mistake: Assuming all regions and peoples freely agreed.
World history

Industrial Revolution

Definition: A long shift toward mechanized production, factories, urbanization, and new transportation systems.

It transformed labour, class, population, empire, technology, and the environment.

Example: Steam power increased factory output and railway transportation.
Common mistake: Treating it as one invention or a brief event.
World history

Imperialism

Definition: The extension of political, economic, or cultural control over other peoples or territories.

It may involve settlement, force, unequal trade, resource extraction, and cultural suppression.

Example: European states claimed territories across Africa and Asia in the nineteenth century.
Common mistake: Ignoring resistance and the agency of colonized peoples.
Canada & World

First World War

Definition: A global war fought mainly from 1914 to 1918 among rival alliances.

Analyze militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism, total war, home fronts, and consequences.

Example: The conflict accelerated industrial production and political change in Canada.
Common mistake: Treating the Sarajevo assassination as the only cause.
Canada & World

Great Depression

Definition: A severe worldwide economic downturn beginning in 1929 and lasting through much of the 1930s.

Study financial instability, falling demand, unemployment, drought, poverty, and policy responses.

Example: Many Canadians experienced unemployment and relied on uneven relief systems.
Common mistake: Assuming the stock-market crash alone explains everything.
Canada & World

Second World War

Definition: A global war from 1939 to 1945 between the Axis and Allied powers.

Core issues include fascism, aggression, the Holocaust, genocide, resistance, total war, and postwar institutions.

Example: Canada contributed forces, production, training, and diplomacy.
Common mistake: Studying battles while ignoring civilians and human rights.
World history

Cold War

Definition: A long geopolitical and ideological rivalry after 1945, especially between the United States and Soviet Union.

It included nuclear deterrence, proxy wars, alliances, propaganda, espionage, and science competition.

Example: The Cuban Missile Crisis showed the danger of nuclear escalation.
Common mistake: Assuming it involved no armed conflict.
Canada

Truth and Reconciliation

Definition: An ongoing process of truth-telling, acknowledgement, repair, and renewed relationships after colonial harms, including residential schools.

Centre survivor testimony, Indigenous rights, the Calls to Action, local treaty relationships, and present responsibilities.

Example: Schools can study local nations and actions responding to the Calls to Action.
Common mistake: Presenting reconciliation as completed or symbolic only.

Geography

Analyze spatial patterns, systems, interrelationships, and sustainability.

Map skills

Latitude and longitude

Definition: A coordinate system used to describe locations on Earth.

Latitude measures north or south of the equator; longitude measures east or west of the prime meridian.

Example: Toronto is near 44° N, 79° W.
Common mistake: Reversing latitude and longitude.
Geospatial skills

Geographic Information System (GIS)

Definition: A system for storing, analyzing, and displaying geographically referenced data.

GIS layers can combine population, land use, transport, hazards, and environmental information.

Example: A city can map flood risk beside homes and emergency routes.
Common mistake: Treating a map as neutral without examining data choices.
Human geography

Population density

Definition: Population divided by land area.

Density does not show how evenly people are distributed inside an area.

Example: Density = population ÷ land area.
Common mistake: Using density as a synonym for total population.
Human geography

Urbanization

Definition: Growth in the share of people living in urban areas and expansion of cities.

Analyze employment, migration, infrastructure, housing, environment, and inequality.

Example: Transit-oriented growth can change housing demand and commuting patterns.
Common mistake: Assuming urban growth has only benefits or only harms.
Environment

Sustainability

Definition: Meeting present needs while maintaining ecological, social, and economic systems for the future.

Good analysis examines trade-offs, equity, scale, evidence, and long-term effects.

Example: A transit plan may reduce emissions while improving access.
Common mistake: Accepting a “green” claim without evidence.

Civics and Citizenship

Understand institutions, rights, responsibilities, policy, and participation.

Government

Democracy

Definition: A system in which people exercise political power directly or through elected representatives.

It also depends on rights, rule of law, participation, accountability, and access to reliable information.

Example: Citizens elect representatives and can organize, petition, and debate public policy.
Common mistake: Reducing democracy to voting once every few years.
Government

Federalism

Definition: A system dividing law-making powers between national and regional governments.

Canada assigns responsibilities to federal and provincial governments; municipalities receive authority from provinces.

Example: Criminal law is federal, while education is mainly provincial.
Common mistake: Assuming one level is always above all others.
Rights

Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms

Definition: Part of the Constitution Act, 1982 protecting specified rights and freedoms.

Rights may involve justified limits, court interpretation, and remedies.

Example: The Charter protects fundamental freedoms and equality rights.
Common mistake: Assuming every private dispute is a Charter case.
Law

Rule of law

Definition: The principle that individuals and governments are governed by publicly known laws applied through lawful institutions.

It supports accountability, due process, predictability, and limits on arbitrary power.

Example: Courts may review government decisions.
Common mistake: Equating rule of law with unquestioning obedience.
Citizenship

Public policy

Definition: A course of action chosen by government to address a public issue.

Compare goals, evidence, cost, feasibility, rights, benefits, harms, and effects on groups.

Example: Evaluate a transit policy for access, cost, emissions, and travel time.
Common mistake: Judging policy only by its stated intention.

Practice prompts

Use these for retrieval and written response.

  1. Compare a primary and secondary source.
  2. Explain two causes and two consequences of an event.
  3. Evaluate a public policy using evidence and three criteria.
  4. Explain how one geographic issue affects groups differently.
