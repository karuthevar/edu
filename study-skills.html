Study & Exam Skills

Practical systems for homework, memory, problem-solving, writing, tests, projects, and academic integrity.

Weekly planning

  • List deadlines and tests
  • Break projects into tasks
  • Schedule difficult work first
  • Add buffer time
  • Review progress weekly

Active recall

Close the notes and retrieve ideas from memory. Use questions, blank-page summaries, and practice problems instead of rereading alone.

Spaced practice

Review over several days or weeks. Short repeated sessions usually build stronger memory than one long cram session.

Problem-solving

  • Identify knowns and unknowns
  • Draw or organize information
  • Select a principle or formula
  • Show steps and units
  • Check reasonableness

Note-taking

Record main ideas, questions, examples, and connections. After class, condense notes into a one-page retrieval sheet.

Test preparation

  • Use the course expectations
  • Mix old and new questions
  • Practise under time limits
  • Keep an error log
  • Sleep and arrive prepared

Academic integrity

Submit your own work, cite sources, distinguish collaboration from copying, and follow teacher rules for calculators and AI tools.

Research credibility

Check author, organization, evidence, date, purpose, citations, and whether independent reliable sources agree.

When stuck

Write the exact step you do understand, identify the first unclear point, consult examples, and ask a focused question.
