Grade 9: Physics

Electricity, energy, motion, forces, and quantitative relationships.

Electric current

Rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.

Example: Measured in amperes (A).

Potential difference

Energy transferred per unit charge between two points.

Example: Measured in volts (V).

Resistance

Opposition to current in a circuit.

Example: Measured in ohms (Ω).

Series circuit

A circuit with one path for current.

Example: Opening one component breaks the whole circuit.

Parallel circuit

A circuit with more than one current path.

Example: Branches can operate independently.

Power

Rate at which energy is transferred.

Example: Measured in watts (W).

Net force

Vector sum of all forces on an object.

Example: Zero net force means no acceleration.

Inertia

Resistance to a change in motion.

Example: Greater mass means greater inertia.

Velocity

Rate of change of displacement.

Example: Includes direction.

Acceleration

Rate of change of velocity.

Example: Can mean speeding up, slowing down, or turning.

Efficiency

Useful output energy divided by input energy.

Example: Usually expressed as a percentage.
🚀

Grade 10: Physics

Optics, energy, momentum, and mechanical systems.

Ray

Idealized line showing the direction light travels.

Example: Used in ray diagrams.

Normal

Line perpendicular to a surface at the point of incidence.

Example: Optics angles are measured from it.

Refraction

Change in direction when light speed changes between media.

Example: A straw appears bent in water.

Index of refraction

Measure of how much light slows in a medium.

Example: Higher index usually means lower speed.

Converging lens

Lens bringing parallel rays toward a focal point.

Example: A convex lens is converging.

Diverging lens

Lens spreading parallel rays apart.

Example: A concave lens is diverging.

Real image

Image formed where rays actually converge.

Example: Can be projected on a screen.

Virtual image

Image formed where rays appear to originate.

Example: Cannot be projected.

Kinetic energy

Energy due to motion.

Example: Depends on speed squared.

Momentum

Product of mass and velocity.

Example: A vector quantity.

Impulse

Change in momentum from force acting over time.

Example: Increasing collision time can lower average force.

Mechanical efficiency

Useful output divided by total input.

Example: Friction lowers efficiency.
🚀

Grade 11: Physics

Kinematics, forces, energy, momentum, waves, and electricity.

Displacement

Vector change in position.

Example: Final position minus initial position.

Instantaneous velocity

Velocity at a specific instant.

Example: Slope of a tangent on a position-time graph.

Uniform acceleration

Constant rate of velocity change.

Example: Produces a linear velocity-time graph.

Free-body diagram

Diagram showing external forces on one object.

Example: Arrows represent vectors.

Normal force

Contact force perpendicular to a surface.

Example: Not always equal to weight.

Tension

Pulling force transmitted by a cable or string.

Example: Acts along the string.

Coefficient of friction

Ratio relating friction and normal force.

Example: μ=Ff/FN.

Impulse

Force-time product equal to momentum change.

Example: J=FΔt=Δp.

Mechanical energy

Sum of kinetic and potential energy.

Example: Conserved without non-conservative work.

Frequency

Number of cycles per second.

Example: Measured in hertz.

Wavelength

Distance between corresponding wave points.

Example: Symbol λ.

Electric field

Force per unit positive test charge.

Example: A vector field.

Electric potential

Potential energy per unit charge.

Example: Measured in volts.

Kirchhoff's laws

Rules for current at junctions and voltage around loops.

Example: Based on conservation.
🚀

Grade 12: Physics

Fields, circular motion, induction, waves, and modern physics.

Centripetal acceleration

Acceleration directed toward the centre of circular motion.

Example: a_c=v²/r.

Centripetal force

Net inward force producing circular motion.

Example: F_c=mv²/r.

Gravitational field

Force per unit mass.

Example: g=F/m.

Electric field

Force per unit positive test charge.

Example: E=F/q.

Magnetic field

Region in which magnetic forces act.

Example: Represented by B.

Electromagnetic induction

Production of emf from changing magnetic flux.

Example: Basis of generators.

Torque

Turning effect of a force.

Example: τ=rFsinθ.

Equilibrium

Zero net force and zero net torque.

Example: No linear or angular acceleration.

Simple harmonic motion

Oscillation with restoring force proportional to displacement.

Example: Ideal spring motion.

Doppler effect

Observed frequency change due to relative motion.

Example: Approaching source raises frequency.

Photon

Quantum of electromagnetic radiation.

Example: E=hf.

Photoelectric effect

Electron emission caused by light above threshold frequency.

Example: Supports quantized light.

Nuclear binding energy

Energy associated with holding a nucleus together.

Example: Related to mass defect.

Half-life

Time for half of a radioactive population to decay statistically.

Example: Exponential process.

Grade 9: Chemistry

Atomic structure, elements, compounds, and reactions.

Atomic number

Number of protons in an atom.

Example: Identifies the element.

Mass number

Total number of protons and neutrons.

Example: Can differ among isotopes.

Isotope

Atoms of one element with different neutron counts.

Example: They have the same atomic number.

Valence electron

Electron in the outermost occupied shell.

Example: Important in bonding.

Periodic table

Arrangement of elements by atomic number and recurring properties.

Example: Groups are columns and periods are rows.

Ionic compound

Compound made of oppositely charged ions.

Example: Often forms from a metal and non-metal.

Molecular compound

Compound in which non-metals share electrons.

Example: Covalent bonds form molecules.

Balanced equation

Equation with equal numbers of each atom on both sides.

Example: Represents conservation of mass.

Synthesis reaction

Two or more substances form one product.

Example: A+B→AB.

Decomposition reaction

One compound breaks into simpler substances.

Example: AB→A+B.

Combustion

Rapid reaction with oxygen that releases energy.

Example: Fuel combustion may form carbon dioxide and water.

Acid

Substance producing an acidic aqueous solution.

Example: Usually pH below 7.

Base

Substance producing a basic aqueous solution.

Example: Usually pH above 7.
🧪

Grade 10: Chemistry

Bonding, naming, reactions, acids, bases, and quantities.

Cation

Positively charged ion.

Example: Forms by losing electrons.

Anion

Negatively charged ion.

Example: Forms by gaining electrons.

Ionic bond

Attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Example: Occurs in ionic compounds.

Covalent bond

Bond involving shared electron pairs.

Example: Common between non-metals.

Polyatomic ion

Charged group of bonded atoms.

Example: Nitrate is NO₃⁻.

Coefficient

Number before a formula in an equation.

Example: Changes amount, not substance identity.

Subscript

Number showing atom ratio in a formula.

Example: H₂O contains two H per O.

Single displacement

One element replaces another in a compound.

Example: A+BC→AC+B.

Double displacement

Ions exchange partners between compounds.

Example: AB+CD→AD+CB.

Neutralization

Acid-base reaction producing water and a salt.

Example: HCl+NaOH→NaCl+H₂O.

pH

Logarithmic measure related to acidity.

Example: Below 7 is acidic in basic classroom use.

Mole

Counting unit for particles.

Example: One mole is 6.022×10²³ entities.

Molar mass

Mass of one mole.

Example: Measured in g/mol.

Concentration

Amount of solute per solution volume.

Example: Often mol/L or percent.
🧪

Grade 11: Chemistry

Bonding, quantities, solutions, gases, and energy.

Electronegativity

Ability of a bonded atom to attract shared electrons.

Example: Predicts bond polarity.

Lewis structure

Diagram showing valence electrons and bonds.

Example: Used for molecules and ions.

VSEPR theory

Model predicting shape from electron-pair repulsion.

Example: Electron groups spread apart.

Intermolecular force

Attraction between molecules.

Example: Affects boiling point and solubility.

Mole

Amount containing Avogadro's number of entities.

Example: 6.022×10²³.

Empirical formula

Simplest whole-number atom ratio.

Example: CH₂O can be empirical.

Stoichiometry

Quantitative relationships from a balanced equation.

Example: Uses mole ratios.

Limiting reagent

Reactant consumed first.

Example: Determines maximum product.

Percent yield

Actual divided by theoretical yield ×100%.

Example: Measures reaction yield.

Molarity

Moles of solute per litre.

Example: mol/L.

Dilution

Lowering concentration by adding solvent.

Example: C₁V₁=C₂V₂.

Ideal gas

Model with negligible particle volume and attractions.

Example: Useful approximation.

Activation energy

Minimum energy barrier for reaction.

Example: Catalysts lower it.

Enthalpy change

Heat change at constant pressure.

Example: Positive endothermic, negative exothermic.

Grade 12: Chemistry

Organic chemistry, energy, rates, equilibrium, acids, and electrochemistry.

Functional group

Atom group controlling organic properties and reactions.

Example: Hydroxyl defines alcohols.

Isomer

Same molecular formula but different structure.

Example: Properties may differ.

Polymer

Large molecule made of repeating monomers.

Example: Polyethylene is a polymer.

Reaction rate

Change in concentration per time.

Example: Affected by temperature and concentration.

Rate law

Equation relating rate to concentrations.

Example: Determined experimentally.

Dynamic equilibrium

Forward and reverse rates are equal.

Example: Concentrations are constant, not necessarily equal.

Equilibrium constant

Ratio of equilibrium concentrations with exponents.

Example: Shows product or reactant favouring.

Le Châtelier's principle

Equilibrium shifts to oppose an imposed change.

Example: Predicts qualitative shifts.

Bronsted-Lowry acid

Proton donor.

Example: HCl donates H⁺.

Bronsted-Lowry base

Proton acceptor.

Example: NH₃ accepts H⁺.

Buffer

Solution resisting pH change.

Example: Contains a conjugate pair.

Oxidation

Loss of electrons or rise in oxidation number.

Example: Occurs at the anode.

Reduction

Gain of electrons or fall in oxidation number.

Example: Occurs at the cathode.

Galvanic cell

Cell producing electrical energy from a spontaneous reaction.

Example: Uses half-cells.

Electrolysis

Electrical energy drives a non-spontaneous reaction.

Example: Occurs in an electrolytic cell.

Grade 9: Biology

Cells, reproduction, ecosystems, and sustainability.

Cell cycle

Sequence of growth, DNA replication, and cell division.

Example: Includes interphase and mitosis.

Mitosis

Division producing two genetically similar daughter cells.

Example: Used for growth and repair.

Chromosome

DNA-containing structure carrying genes.

Example: Duplicates before mitosis.

Gene

Segment of DNA associated with a product or trait.

Example: Genes are inherited.

Differentiation

Process by which a cell becomes specialized.

Example: Produces nerve and muscle cells.

Ecosystem

Organisms interacting with one another and the environment.

Example: Energy flows and matter cycles.

Biotic factor

Living component of an ecosystem.

Example: Plants and predators are biotic.

Abiotic factor

Non-living environmental component.

Example: Water and temperature are abiotic.

Carrying capacity

Largest population an environment can sustain.

Example: Limited by resources.

Trophic level

Feeding position in a food chain or web.

Example: Producers occupy the first level.

Bioaccumulation

Build-up of a substance in one organism over time.

Example: Persistent pollutants can collect in tissues.

Biomagnification

Increase of pollutant concentration at higher trophic levels.

Example: Top predators may contain the most.

Biodiversity

Variety of genes, species, and ecosystems.

Example: Can support resilience.
🌱

Grade 10: Biology

Cells, tissues, body systems, disease, and homeostasis.

Specialized cell

Cell adapted for a particular function.

Example: Red blood cells transport oxygen.

Epithelial tissue

Tissue covering surfaces and lining cavities.

Example: Skin includes epithelial tissue.

Connective tissue

Tissue supporting, binding, or transporting.

Example: Bone and blood are connective.

Muscle tissue

Tissue producing movement through contraction.

Example: Cardiac muscle forms the heart.

Nervous tissue

Tissue transmitting electrical signals.

Example: Found in the nervous system.

Circulatory system

Heart, blood, and vessels transporting materials.

Example: Supplies oxygen and nutrients.

Respiratory system

Organs involved in gas exchange.

Example: Alveoli exchange gases.

Digestive system

Organs digesting food and absorbing nutrients.

Example: Small intestine absorbs nutrients.

Excretory system

System removing metabolic wastes and regulating fluids.

Example: Kidneys filter blood.

Pathogen

Disease-causing biological agent.

Example: Viruses and some bacteria are pathogens.

Cancer

Disease involving uncontrolled abnormal cell division.

Example: Mutations can disrupt cell-cycle control.

Homeostasis

Regulation of stable internal conditions.

Example: Body temperature is regulated.

Negative feedback

Response reducing a deviation from a set range.

Example: Sweating opposes overheating.

Grade 11: Biology

Evolution, genetics, diversity, plants, and animals.

Taxonomy

Science of classifying organisms.

Example: Uses hierarchical categories.

Phylogeny

Evolutionary history and relationships.

Example: Shown with evolutionary trees.

Natural selection

Differential reproduction based on heritable variation.

Example: Changes allele frequencies.

Allele

Alternative form of a gene.

Example: Individuals can carry different alleles.

Genotype

Genetic makeup for a trait.

Example: Example Aa.

Phenotype

Observable trait influenced by genes and environment.

Example: Example flower colour.

Meiosis

Division producing haploid gametes.

Example: Creates variation.

Crossing over

DNA exchange between homologous chromosomes.

Example: Occurs in meiosis I.

Mutation

Change in DNA sequence.

Example: May be neutral, harmful, or beneficial.

Enzyme

Biological catalyst.

Example: Lowers activation energy.

Active site

Region where substrate binds.

Example: Determines specificity.

Transpiration

Water loss from plant leaves.

Example: Helps pull water upward.

Tropism

Directional plant growth response.

Example: Phototropism responds to light.
🌱

Grade 12: Biology

Biochemistry, metabolism, molecular genetics, and homeostasis.

Macromolecule

Large biological molecule such as protein or nucleic acid.

Example: Built from characteristic subunits.

ATP

Immediate cellular energy-transfer molecule.

Example: Hydrolysis powers cellular work.

Cellular respiration

Pathways oxidizing fuel to produce ATP.

Example: Includes glycolysis and aerobic stages.

Chemiosmosis

ATP production driven by ion flow through ATP synthase.

Example: Occurs across membranes.

Photosynthesis

Conversion of light energy to chemical energy.

Example: Includes light reactions and carbon fixation.

DNA replication

Semiconservative copying of DNA.

Example: Occurs before division.

Transcription

Synthesis of RNA from DNA template.

Example: Catalyzed by RNA polymerase.

Translation

Synthesis of polypeptide from mRNA.

Example: Occurs at ribosomes.

Codon

Three-base mRNA sequence specifying an amino acid or stop.

Example: Read during translation.

Gene regulation

Control of gene expression.

Example: Enables specialization.

Hormone

Chemical messenger acting on target cells.

Example: Binds receptors.

Neuron

Excitable signaling cell.

Example: Uses electrical and chemical communication.

Population genetics

Study of allele frequencies in populations.

Example: Links genetics and evolution.

Evolution

Change in inherited population characteristics over generations.

Example: Driven by several mechanisms.

Grade 9: Earth & Space

Astronomy, climate, Earth systems, and exploration.

Astronomical unit

Average Earth–Sun distance.

Example: About 150 million kilometres.

Light-year

Distance light travels in one year.

Example: It is a distance, not a time.

Galaxy

Gravitationally bound system of stars, gas, and dust.

Example: The Milky Way is our galaxy.

Nebula

Large cloud of gas and dust in space.

Example: Some are star-forming regions.

Star

Luminous plasma sphere powered by fusion.

Example: The Sun is a star.

Orbit

Curved path around another body due to gravity.

Example: Earth orbits the Sun.

Electromagnetic spectrum

Range of electromagnetic radiation.

Example: Includes radio, visible, and X-rays.

Greenhouse gas

Gas that absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation.

Example: Carbon dioxide and methane are examples.

Albedo

Fraction of incoming light reflected by a surface.

Example: Ice has higher albedo than ocean.

Space probe

Uncrewed spacecraft used to collect data.

Example: Probes explore planets.

Satellite

Object orbiting another body.

Example: May be natural or artificial.
🌎

Grade 10: Earth & Climate

Climate systems, energy balance, and human impacts.

Climate change

Long-term change in climate patterns.

Example: Includes temperature and precipitation changes.

Radiative forcing

Change in Earth's energy balance caused by a factor.

Example: Greenhouse gases can create positive forcing.

Carbon cycle

Movement of carbon among Earth systems.

Example: Combustion transfers carbon to the atmosphere.

Carbon sink

Reservoir absorbing more carbon than it releases.

Example: Forests can be sinks.

Carbon source

Process releasing carbon.

Example: Fossil-fuel combustion is a source.

Feedback loop

Process amplifying or reducing an initial change.

Example: Ice-albedo feedback can amplify warming.

Mitigation

Action reducing causes of climate change.

Example: Renewable energy can reduce emissions.

Adaptation

Adjustment reducing climate harm.

Example: Flood-resistant infrastructure is adaptation.

Renewable energy

Energy from replenishing sources.

Example: Solar, wind, and hydro.

Life-cycle assessment

Evaluation of impacts from production through disposal.

Example: Used to compare products.
🌎

Grade 11: Environmental Science

Population, resources, ecosystems, and environmental decisions.

Ecological footprint

Estimated productive area supporting consumption.

Example: A sustainability indicator.

Population growth rate

Population change over time.

Example: Affected by births, deaths, and migration.

Exponential growth

Growth proportional to current population.

Example: J-shaped under ideal conditions.

Logistic growth

Growth slowing near carrying capacity.

Example: S-shaped curve.

Indicator species

Species reflecting environmental quality.

Example: Sensitive species can reveal pollution.

Eutrophication

Nutrient enrichment causing excess growth and oxygen depletion.

Example: Often affects lakes.

Bioremediation

Use of organisms to reduce pollutants.

Example: Some microbes degrade contaminants.

Environmental impact assessment

Evaluation of likely project effects.

Example: Supports decisions.

Precautionary principle

Preventive action when serious risk is plausible despite uncertainty.

Example: Used in environmental policy.

Astronomy, climate, energy, and scientific decisions.

Blackbody radiation

Idealized emission determined by temperature.

Example: Used in stellar and climate models.

Redshift

Shift of spectral lines toward longer wavelength.

Example: Can indicate recession.

Spectroscopy

Study of matter-radiation interactions.

Example: Reveals composition and motion.

Stellar evolution

Changes in a star through its lifetime.

Example: Depends strongly on initial mass.

Nuclear fusion

Combining light nuclei and releasing energy.

Example: Powers stars.

Climate sensitivity

Long-term temperature response to forcing.

Example: Includes feedbacks.

Carbon budget

Cumulative emissions compatible with a temperature target.

Example: Used in climate planning.

Systems model

Simplified representation of interacting components.

Example: Can include stocks and flows.

Uncertainty

Quantified limitation in knowledge or prediction.

Example: Does not mean no evidence.

Risk assessment

Evaluation of likelihood and consequence.

Example: Supports decisions.

Laws, Theories & Principles

Grade 9

Ohm's Law

V=IR for an ohmic conductor under constant physical conditions.

Meaning: Connects voltage, current, and resistance.
Grade 9

Conservation of Energy

Energy is transferred or transformed, not created or destroyed.

Meaning: Input equals total output in a closed accounting.
Grade 9

Conservation of Mass

Atoms are rearranged during reactions; mass is conserved in a closed system.

Meaning: Balanced equations represent this.
Grade 9

Newton's First Law

Motion remains unchanged unless a net external force acts.

Meaning: Explains inertia.
Grade 9

Newton's Second Law

Net force equals mass times acceleration.

Meaning: Fnet=ma.
Grade 9

Newton's Third Law

Interaction forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction on different objects.

Meaning: Useful for propulsion.
Grade 9

Law of Reflection

Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection.

Meaning: Angles are measured from the normal.
Grade 10

Law of Reflection

θi=θr.

Meaning: Angles measured from the normal.
Grade 10

Snell's Law

n₁sinθ₁=n₂sinθ₂.

Meaning: Describes refraction.
Grade 10

Conservation of Energy

Total energy is conserved in an isolated system.

Meaning: Mechanical energy may become thermal.
Grade 10

Conservation of Momentum

Total momentum is constant in an isolated system.

Meaning: Useful for collisions.
Grade 10

Conservation of Mass

Mass is conserved in a closed chemical system.

Meaning: Requires balanced equations.
Grade 10

Periodic Law

Properties recur when elements are ordered by atomic number.

Meaning: Explains periodic trends.
Grade 10

Cell Theory

Cells are basic units of life and arise from existing cells.

Meaning: Foundation of biology.
Grade 11

Newton's Laws

Connect force, mass, acceleration, and interactions.

Meaning: Foundation of mechanics.
Grade 11

Conservation of Momentum

Total momentum remains constant in an isolated system.

Meaning: Applied to collisions.
Grade 11

Conservation of Mechanical Energy

KE+PE remains constant when only conservative forces act.

Meaning: Friction converts mechanical energy.
Grade 11

Hooke's Law

Spring force is proportional and opposite to displacement.

Meaning: F=−kx.
Grade 11

Wave relation

Wave speed equals frequency times wavelength.

Meaning: v=fλ.
Grade 11

Ideal Gas Law

PV=nRT for an ideal gas model.

Meaning: Links state variables.
Grade 11

Hess's Law

Total enthalpy change is path independent.

Meaning: Reaction enthalpies can be added.
Grade 11

Mendelian segregation

Allele pairs separate during gamete formation.

Meaning: Explains simple inheritance.
Grade 11

Hardy-Weinberg principle

Allele frequencies stay constant under ideal assumptions.

Meaning: Evolutionary baseline.
Grade 12

Universal Gravitation

F=Gm₁m₂/r².

Meaning: Attractive force between masses.
Grade 12

Coulomb's Law

F=kq₁q₂/r² in magnitude.

Meaning: Force between point charges.
Grade 12

Faraday's Law

Changing magnetic flux induces emf.

Meaning: Foundation of generators.
Grade 12

Lenz's Law

Induced current opposes the change producing it.

Meaning: Sets direction.
Grade 12

Conservation of Energy and Momentum

Total energy and momentum are conserved in isolated systems.

Meaning: Includes nuclear contexts.
Grade 12

Planck relation

Photon energy is proportional to frequency.

Meaning: E=hf.
Grade 12

First Law of Thermodynamics

Internal-energy change follows heat and work transfer.

Meaning: Energy accounting.
Grade 12

Le Châtelier's Principle

Equilibrium shifts in response to disturbance.

Meaning: Qualitative prediction.
Grade 12

Central Dogma

Genetic information generally flows DNA→RNA→protein.

Meaning: Core molecular biology framework.
Grade 12

Hardy-Weinberg Principle

Expected genotype frequencies under ideal assumptions.

Meaning: p²+2pq+q²=1.
