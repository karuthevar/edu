Economics

Understand choices, markets, the broader economy, trade, indicators, and policy.

Foundations

Scarcity

Definition: Resources are limited while wants and possible uses are numerous.

Scarcity forces choices and creates opportunity costs.

Example: One public dollar cannot fund two projects at the same time.
Common mistake: Confusing scarcity with total absence.
Foundations

Opportunity cost

Definition: The value of the best alternative given up when a choice is made.

It can include time, resources, experience, and money.

Example: The opportunity cost of studying Friday may be the event you miss.
Common mistake: Listing every alternative instead of the best forgone one.
Markets

Demand

Definition: Quantities consumers are willing and able to buy at various prices during a period.

Price changes move along a demand curve; non-price factors shift it.

Example: Higher income may increase demand for a normal good.
Common mistake: Calling a price-caused quantity change a demand shift.
Markets

Supply

Definition: Quantities producers are willing and able to sell at various prices during a period.

Input costs, technology, expectations, sellers, and policy can shift supply.

Example: Lower production costs may shift supply right.
Common mistake: Assuming greater supply guarantees greater profit.
Markets

Market equilibrium

Definition: The price and quantity where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

Shortages put upward pressure on price; surpluses put downward pressure.

Example: At equilibrium, planned purchases equal planned sales.
Common mistake: Assuming equilibrium is automatically fair.
Markets

Price elasticity of demand

Definition: Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a percentage change in price.

Elastic demand changes proportionally more; inelastic demand changes less.

Example: Goods with close substitutes often have more elastic demand.
Common mistake: Treating slope and elasticity as identical.
Macroeconomics

Gross domestic product (GDP)

Definition: Market value of final goods and services produced within a country during a period.

GDP does not measure all well-being, unpaid work, equality, or environmental quality.

Example: Count final bread or value added, not every sale in the chain.
Common mistake: Double-counting intermediate goods.
Macroeconomics

Inflation

Definition: A sustained increase in the general price level.

It reduces purchasing power and is commonly estimated from a price index.

Example: A $100 representative basket becomes about $103 after 3% inflation.
Common mistake: Calling one item’s price rise general inflation.
Personal finance

Compound interest

Definition: Interest calculated on principal plus accumulated interest.

Rate, time, compounding frequency, and fees all matter.

Example: A = P(1+i)^n.
Common mistake: Comparing borrowing options by advertised rate alone.
Personal finance

Diversification

Definition: Spreading investments across assets to reduce concentration risk.

It reduces some risk but cannot guarantee profit or eliminate market risk.

Example: Many sectors are less concentrated than one company.
Common mistake: Assuming many holdings are diversified when they share the same risk.

Accounting and business formulas

Core relationships used in introductory business courses.

Accounting equation

Assets = Liabilities + Owner’s Equity

Every transaction must preserve balance.

Profit

Profit = Revenue − Expenses

Revenue alone does not show profitability.

Break-even quantity

Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost per Unit)

The contribution margin must be positive.
